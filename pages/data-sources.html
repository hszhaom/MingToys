PetStorie Data Sources

PetStorie is a general dog breed research site. Our guides are designed to help readers compare breeds, understand daily care trade-offs, and prepare better questions for veterinarians, trainers, breeders, rescues, and shelters.

Source Categories We Use

  • Breed standards and breed descriptions from recognized kennel organizations and breed clubs.
  • Veterinary education resources for common breed health concerns, warning signs, weight control, and preventive care.
  • Animal welfare, shelter, and rescue guidance about adoption fit, behavior, enrichment, and responsible ownership.
  • Responsible breeder and breed-community education materials when they explain health testing, temperament, and owner fit.
  • Grooming, training, insurance, and cost-planning references used to frame realistic owner questions rather than exact quotes.
  • Recurring owner-reported patterns in public breed discussions are used cautiously as context, not as proof of medical or behavioral facts.

Named Reference Organizations

Individual guides now list the pages consulted for that article. The following organizations form the core reference set. A link in this list does not imply that the organization endorses PetStorie.

  • American Kennel Club breed directory for recognized breed profiles, size, history, and general traits.
  • United Kennel Club breed standards when a breed is not represented in the AKC directory or a second standard is useful.
  • Orthopedic Foundation for Animals CHIC browse-by-breed tool for health-screening context.
  • ASPCA dog-care guidance for general welfare and routine-care context.
  • North American Pet Health Insurance Association industry data for insurance-market context used by the cost-planning tool.
  • National parent clubs listed on individual breed guides for breed-specific health, temperament, and ownership material.

How Article Source Lists Work

A source appears on an article only when it was used to check material on that page. Each entry names the organization, page, URL, and access date. The source list supports the general breed-level statements in the guide; it does not certify the health or behavior of an individual dog.

Visual Materials

PetStorie uses locally hosted licensed photographs where an article provides an image credit. New editorial visuals may be AI-assisted illustrations and are labelled on the relevant guide. They are used to show general breed appearance or a care setting, never as proof of an editor's personal ownership, a veterinarian's assessment, a rescue case, or an individual dog's health or temperament.

Sources may disagree or describe a broad range. PetStorie keeps that uncertainty visible instead of turning a planning range into a guarantee. When a claim concerns a symptom, diagnosis, treatment, or individual safety decision, the article directs the reader to a veterinarian or qualified behavior professional.

Owner-reported sections are qualitative editorial syntheses. They are not presented as a statistically representative survey or as interviews conducted by PetStorie unless the page publishes the method and participant information needed to verify that claim.

Tool Methodology

The Dog Fit Score Cards use whole-number editorial levels from 1 to 5. They are not survey percentages or medical risk predictions. The page publishes the rubric and explains how the direction changes between a fit score and a demand or risk score.

The Dog Cost Calculator uses broad United States planning ranges in 2026 US dollars. It shows the monthly categories included in the estimate and excludes purchase price, licensing, a specific emergency, and treatment of a diagnosed condition. Local quotes should replace every category before an owner makes a financial decision.

How We Use Data

PetStorie treats breed facts as planning ranges. A guide may describe a dog as high energy, heavy shedding, expensive to groom, heat sensitive, or difficult for first-time owners, but those labels are starting points. Individual dogs vary by genetics, age, health, training, environment, early socialization, and owner routine.

When we discuss health risks, the purpose is to help readers ask better questions and notice when professional care may be needed. We do not diagnose conditions, recommend medication, replace veterinary exams, or claim that every dog of a breed will develop a listed condition.

Personal dog ownership experience may influence which practical questions PetStorie highlights, such as grooming routines, daily exercise, cost surprises, and household fit. It is not used as proof for medical claims, breed-wide behavior claims, or health-screening recommendations.

Tool examples are illustrative planning scenarios unless they are identified as a documented first-hand owner note. They do not represent a survey, a case study, or the personal history of the editor. This distinction keeps examples useful without turning generated scenarios into evidence.

What We Avoid

  • Invented statistics, fake expert quotes, fabricated author credentials, or unsupported medical claims.
  • Absolute promises that a breed is always safe, hypoallergenic, healthy, easy, or suitable for every home.
  • Single-source claims for sensitive topics such as disease risk, aggression, insurance restrictions, or cost.
  • Copying source text into articles instead of writing original explanations for readers.

Corrections

If a page appears outdated, unclear, or inconsistent with a reliable source, please report the page URL and concern through the contact page. We review correction requests for factual accuracy, reader safety, source clarity, and wording that may overstate certainty.
