Editorial standards

Editorial Policy

PetStorie publishes informational dog breed content for readers who want realistic, research-driven guidance before choosing or living with a breed.

Editorial Ownership

PetStorie articles are written and maintained by the PetStorie editorial team. The site does not currently claim veterinary review, certified trainer review, breed-club endorsement, or paid expert authorship unless a specific page states otherwise. Our responsibility is to make general breed research clearer, more balanced, and easier to verify.

Every guide should help a reader understand practical ownership questions: daily routine, temperament, exercise, grooming, training difficulty, common health considerations, cost pressure, household fit, and situations where a dog may not be a good match.

How Articles Are Built

  • Each guide is written around a specific reader question or search intent, such as breed temperament, owner fit, grooming, or exercise needs.
  • Content aims to balance strengths, limitations, and common ownership trade-offs rather than present breeds as universally suitable.
  • Pages are revised when structure, clarity, or policy compliance needs improvement.
  • Breed pages are checked for practical sections such as owner fit, exercise, grooming, health cautions, training, costs, pros and cons, and FAQ.

Data Sources and Research Notes

PetStorie uses a source-first research process for breed facts and owner guidance. We compare information across recognized kennel organizations, breed standards, responsible breeder and rescue education materials, veterinary education resources, animal welfare guidance, and practical owner-care references. We do not treat a single source as enough when a claim affects health, safety, cost, or owner suitability.

Breed size, lifespan, grooming level, exercise needs, trainability, shedding, and cost ranges are presented as general planning ranges, not guarantees for an individual dog. Lines, age, sex, health history, region, breeder practices, diet, insurance, grooming market, and training environment can all change the real outcome.

A fuller explanation of the source categories we use is available on the PetStorie data sources page.

Core breed guides and monetized comparison pages include a page-level source list with the organization, referenced page, URL, and access date. A source is listed only when it was consulted for that page. General source categories do not replace article-level references for breed facts, health-screening context, or cost methodology.

Owner-reported reality sections are editorial summaries of recurring practical issues described in public breed discussions, breed-community education, rescue guidance, and long-term care material. PetStorie does not present those summaries as a formal survey, a local interview project, or proof that the editorial team has personally owned every breed. A specific interview count will appear only when the participants and method can be documented.

Originality and Usefulness Standards

PetStorie pages are intended to add practical context, not simply repeat breed stereotypes. A useful page should explain the trade-off behind a label. For example, "low shedding" should also explain grooming costs, "family friendly" should explain supervision and child handling, and "guardian" should explain visitor management and fencing.

When pages are improved, the goal is to make them more specific, less formulaic, and more helpful for an owner making a real decision. We avoid hidden text, copied articles, fabricated credentials, fake reviews, and exaggerated claims about health, behavior, or suitability.

Health and Safety Content

PetStorie does not publish veterinary diagnoses or treatment plans. Health sections are written conservatively and are meant to help readers recognize common breed concerns, ask better questions, and know when veterinary advice is appropriate.

When a topic touches disease, symptoms, medication, emergencies, or unusual behavior, the safest next step is consultation with a licensed veterinarian or qualified behavior professional.

Author Notes and Byline Policy

Articles use the byline "PetStorie Editorial Team" because they are maintained as site guides rather than personal essays. This keeps accountability with the site and avoids inventing individual identities or credentials. If PetStorie later adds named expert contributors, their credentials, role, and review scope will be shown on the relevant pages.

Corrections and Updates

We update content when we find factual errors, unclear wording, broken links, outdated structure, or policy issues that could affect reader trust. If you notice a problem, please use the contact information on the contact page.

Update work may include rewriting a page for clarity, adding missing cautions, improving internal links, correcting an image reference, or narrowing a recommendation that sounded too broad. Pages may also be updated when search intent changes or a topic collection needs more complete comparison information.

Automation and Editorial Judgment

PetStorie may use writing and editing tools to organize drafts, check structure, or improve readability. Final published pages are expected to follow the site's editorial rules: no invented expertise, no unsupported medical advice, no keyword stuffing, and no content that exists only to fill space around ads.

Structure and editing tools do not receive an author byline and do not count as expert review. The editorial team remains responsible for checking links, removing unsupported precision, keeping source lists current, and deciding whether a page has enough independent value to carry advertising.

Advertising Independence

Advertising, when present, does not determine which breeds are covered or how a breed is described. Editorial content is intended to remain independent from ad placement and monetization.

PetStorie does not sell guaranteed breed rankings, paid medical claims, or sponsored promises about suitability for a home, family, or health outcome.
