Small Dog Breeds

Small dogs can fit apartments, travel easily, and cost less to feed, but they are not automatically low-maintenance. Many need serious grooming, patient training, dental care, protection from rough handling, or careful heat management. Use this page to compare the trade-offs before picking a tiny companion.

Quick Comparison

Breed Best For Watch For
Chihuahua Owners who want a loyal, tiny companion with a bold personality. Fragility, dental disease, cold sensitivity, and overprotective behavior.
Toy Poodle People who enjoy training, grooming routines, and a clever small dog. Professional grooming costs, sensitivity, and daily mental work.
Pomeranian Homes that like a lively spitz personality in a tiny body. Barking, heavy coat care, luxating patellas, and trachea concerns.
Shih Tzu Owners wanting a mellow, affectionate lap companion. Flat-face breathing limits, heat sensitivity, and coat maintenance.
Papillon Active owners who want a tiny dog with sports-dog brains. Fragile bones, high mental needs, and more exercise than expected.

How to Choose a Small Dog

Start with handling and lifestyle. A fragile toy breed may be a poor match for toddlers or rough play, while a vocal watchdog type may frustrate close neighbors. Coat type matters too. A low-shedding dog can still need frequent brushing and paid grooming, while a short-coated dog may shed less visibly but still need dental care and training.

Health should be part of the decision from the start. Small breeds often need extra attention to teeth, knees, trachea, eyes, and weight control. If you are comparing several tiny breeds, read the full guides above and ask breeders direct questions about health testing and temperament.
